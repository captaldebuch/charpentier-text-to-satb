Trace the eight measures

Measures 1–3 and 5–7 cite a pattern, its time signature and its location in the source score. The engine composes the cadences in measures 4 and 8. Scale steps start from the first note (0) and durations are in quarter notes.

    How the music is built

    1. The engine identifies words and estimates their length from groups of vowels. Each word begins on a note and may extend over several notes. This is an approximation of how the words might be sung.
    2. For six of the eight measures, the engine retrieves a pattern that fits the note count and meter. It favors longer notes at points of emphasis and small intervals between bars. A half cadence at measure 4 leaves the first phrase on the dominant. The chosen cadence ends the second phrase at measure 8.
    3. The engine harmonizes the soprano by comparing ways to voice the alto, tenor and bass. It considers chord connections, spacing and the motion of each voice. After composition it reads all four parts and counts features such as parallel fifths and octaves.

    This is generation guided by musical pattern retrieval, a broad sense of retrieval-augmented generation (RAG). The search uses scale steps and note durations. The demo runs on rules without a language model, vector embeddings or neural training.

    Key and meter

    Within the same mode, choosing a new tonic transposes all four voices together, preserving the words and rhythms. For major keys, the number in parentheses gives the shift in semitones from C major. Switching between major and minor recalculates the harmony.

    With the same draw, 4/4 and 2/2 give the same arrangement. The notation groups four quarter notes as four beats or as two half-note beats. In 2/4, 3/4 or 6/8, the engine composes a fresh study for the chosen meter. Count three quarter-note beats in 3/4 and two dotted-quarter beats in 6/8.
